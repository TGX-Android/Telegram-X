# Threat model

## What this project does and where untrusted input enters

Telegram X is an official alternative Telegram client for Android (`org.thunderdog.challegram` in releases), written in Java and Kotlin, with native C, C++ and Rust code for media and calls. [TDLib](https://github.com/tdlib/td) does MTProto and encryption, secret chats included, the local database, downloads and the parsing of what the servers send. The app gets it as `TdApi` objects from the prebuilt bundle of the `tdlib` submodule (`TdApi.java` and a stripped `libtdjni.so` with OpenSSL for each NDK, API level and ABI). Telegram X draws, decodes and acts on those objects, handles links, intents of other apps and files, and runs calls. What other people send stays untrusted after TDLib has parsed it.

Untrusted input enters through the places below. Paths of Java and Kotlin are under `app/src/main/java/org/thunderdog/challegram/`, other paths start at the root of the repository.

- **Messages of users, bots and channels**: text with entities (offsets, lengths, nesting, custom emoji, hidden URLs, `pre` code blocks) laid out by `util/text/Text.java` and `util/text/TextEntityMessage.java`; the code of a `pre` block, in the language its entity names or `util/text/CodeLanguageDetector.java` guesses, highlighted without a tap by libprisma with Boost.Regex in `app/jni/syntax.cpp` (`util/text/CodeHighlighter.java`, `N.highlightCode`); the content types of `data/TGMessage*.java`; names, titles, captions and texts wherever they are drawn, also in notifications (`telegram/TdlibNotification*.java`) and the chat list. Telegram X has no LaTeX rendering, and rich messages, invoices, stories and gifts of stars (`MessageGift`, `MessageUpgradedGift` and their kin) show as unsupported (`TGMessage.valueOf`); Premium gift codes and giveaways are shown and parsed by `data/TGMessageGift.java` and `data/TGMessageGiveaway.java`.
- **Instant View and link previews**: page blocks (`data/PageBlock*.java`, `ui/InstantViewController.java`), embedded HTML of a page loaded with JavaScript on and the `TelegramWebviewProxy` bridge (`data/PageBlockMedia.processWebView`, `widget/PageBlockWrapView.java`), previews of web pages (`data/TGWebPage.java`) and embedded players (`data/EmbeddedService.java`, `component/preview/WebViewPreviewLayout.java`). HTML5 games of bots run in `ui/GameController.java`, whose page can call `v/WebViewProxy.java`. Mini apps are not implemented: their links show as unsupported.
- **Stickers, custom emoji, GIFs, videos and voice**, decoded by native code of the app, mostly without a tap: `.tgs` (gzipped Lottie JSON) by tlottie and WebM by FFmpeg with libvpx in `app/jni/gif.cpp` (`loader/gif/GifActor.java`, `N.createLottieDecoder`, `N.createDecoder`), with frames cached by LZ4 (`loader/gif/LottieCache.java`); WebP by the bundled libwebp in `app/jni/image.c` (`loader/ImageReader.java`, `N.loadWebpImage`); Opus voice messages by opusfile and ogg in `app/jni/voice.c` (`core/Audio.java`). Music and videos play in androidx-media (ExoPlayer) with the FFmpeg, FLAC, Opus and VP9 extensions, whose JNI the app builds from patched copies (`ffmpeg_jni.cc`, `flac_jni.cc`, `flac_parser.cc`, `opus_jni.cc`, `vpx_jni.cc`, made by `PatchJetpackMediaTask`). FFmpeg is configured by `buildSrc/src/main/kotlin/tgx/gradle/task/BuildFfmpegTask.kt`: the decoders h264, mpeg4, gif, alac, aac and libvpx_vp9, the demuxers mov, matroska and gif, no network.
- **Backgrounds, themes and language packs**: patterns of chat backgrounds, gzipped SVG drawn by `loader/svg/SvgRender.java` (`theme/TGBackground.java`, `data/WallpaperComponent.java`); `.tgx-theme` files sent as documents, parsed by `TdlibUi.readCustomTheme`; language packs from links, which anyone can make on Telegram's translation platform (`TdlibUi.installLanguage`, strings formatted by `core/Lang.java`).
- **Links**: `tg:` links and `t.me`, `telegram.me`, `telegram.dog` and `tx.me` from messages, QR codes, other apps and browsers. TDLib parses them (`TdlibUi.openTelegramUrl`), the app acts on each type in `TdlibUi.openInternalLinkType`: e.g. proxy links, which `TdlibUi.openProxyAlert` confirms before `Settings.addOrUpdateProxy` stores them with their secrets, and language packs, invite links, bot starts, phone numbers. Other links open in a browser: a URL written in the text opens as it reads, while a link that the text hides (a text URL, `ViewController.openLinkAlert`), a URL button or a sponsored link asks first when its host isn't trusted (`UrlOpenParameters.requireOpenPrompt`, `TdlibUi.openUrlImpl`); login URLs of bots ask before TDLib shares the account with a site.
- **Files and their names**: documents of others are saved to Downloads under the name TDLib suggests, refused if it has a separator (`TD.saveToDownloadsImpl`), and opened by other apps through `FileProvider.java` with the paths of `app/src/main/res/xml/provider_paths.xml` (`tool/Intents.openFile`); text files of the types of `tool/TGMimeType.java`, e.g. code and Markdown, open in the app's own viewer, `ui/TextController.java`; APKs go to the package installer.
- **Other apps**: `MainActivity` is exported for `MAIN`, `SEND` and `SEND_MULTIPLE` of any type, and `VIEW` of the links above; its `handleIntent` also takes the actions of `tool/Intents.java` (open a chat, the main screen, logs, the screen of a call, the player, live location, and "Call Back" of a notification of a call, which calls the user back, after the passcode, when the intent has the token of the app's notification, `Intents.isCallBackTrusted`), and `MAIN` with `NOTIFICATION_PREFERENCES` from the system's settings, which opens the settings of the channel its extras name (`MainActivity.openNotificationSettings`). Shared streams are resolved by `U.tryResolveFilePath` and `MainController.addShareUri`, and must never be the app's own files (`U.isInternalUri`); a shared vCard is parsed in `MainController.shareIntentImplSingle`. Files that pickers of other apps return come through `U.resolveSharedFilePath`. The keyboard can commit images into the message field (`component/chat/InputView.java`), which must never be the app's own files either (`U.isInternalUri`, as for shares). Also exported: the sync adapter `sync/SyncService`. Actions of notifications come back by `PendingIntent` to the receivers of `receiver/`, which aren't exported.
- **Pushes**: Firebase data (`service/PushHandler.kt`, `service/PushProcessor.java`) goes to TDLib, which decrypts it; the notification shows names and texts of others.
- **Calls**: private calls run tgcalls and WebRTC of the forks in `app/jni/tgvoip/third_party/` through `app/jni/tgvoip/tgvoip.cpp` (`voip/TgCallsController.java`, `telegram/CallManager.java`, `service/TGCallService.java`). Signaling data of the other side reaches the native code by `processIncomingSignalingData`, its media by the network.
- **Channels of the project**, found by username: APK updates from `Telegram.UPDATES_CHANNEL` (`Tdlib.findUpdateFile`, `util/AppUpdater.java`; off in experimental builds such as this image's), and emoji and icon packs from `Telegram.RESOURCES_CHANNEL` (`Tdlib.fetchCloudSettings`; emoji packs are unzipped in `emoji/Emoji.java`).

Assets: the session of each account (the files of TDLib in the app's private storage), messages and media, secret chats, contacts, location, the microphone and camera, proxies with their secrets and the hashes of passcodes (`unsorted/Settings.java` in LevelDB of `vkryl/leveldb`, `unsorted/Passcode.java`, `telegram/ChatPasscode.java`), and logs (`Log.java`, `files/logs`, shared only when the user asks).

## Components that matter most / least

Most, roughly in order:

1. Native code fed by content of others without a tap: `app/jni/gif.cpp`, `app/jni/image.c`, `app/jni/voice.c`, `app/jni/syntax.cpp`, the JNI of the androidx-media extensions, and the code of FFmpeg, libvpx, libwebp, opus, opusfile, ogg, FLAC, libyuv, LZ4, libprisma with Boost.Regex and tlottie (Rust with an `unsafe` C API) as the app configures and calls them.
2. Calls: `app/jni/tgvoip/` with tgcalls and WebRTC as reached by the other party of a call.
3. Java that parses or lays out content of others: entities and text layout, the guess of the language of code, Instant View, link previews, SVG patterns, theme files, language packs, vCards.
4. The boundary with other apps: `MainActivity.handleIntent`, sharing into the app, files from pickers and keyboards, `FileProvider`, exported components, `PendingIntent`s.
5. Links and web content: dispatch of internal links, confirmations of proxies, external links and login URLs, and the web views with their bridges.

In scope also: the libraries of the project under `vkryl/` (`X-Core`, `X-Android`, `tdlib-utils`, LevelDB) and `app/jni/third_party/jni-utils`, and `extension/hms` (pushes of Huawei, not built here).

Least: drawing, animation, charts, settings screens and themes built into the app, which don't take input of others; the build tooling (`buildSrc/`, `scripts/`, Gradle files) unless it makes the built app unsafe; the flavors for old Android versions (`legacy`, `lollipop`, `marshmallow`, `nop`, `qrs`), which share the code of `latest`, as far as they differ only by what the old system does.

Out of scope:

- TDLib, MTProto, the encryption of cloud and secret chats, and the Telegram servers: report them to [tdlib/td](https://github.com/tdlib/td) and the [Telegram bug bounty](https://core.telegram.org/bug-bounty), as `docs/SECURITY.md` says. The prebuilt TDLib under `tdlib/` goes there too, and the OpenSSL beside it to OpenSSL.
- Bugs of third-party libraries that Telegram X doesn't reach, or reaches only through input it never passes; the same bug reached through Telegram X, or a library misused by it, is in scope.
- Components of Android and Google: Android System WebView, the hardware decoders of MediaCodec, `BitmapFactory`, `MediaMetadataRetriever`, Google Play services, Firebase.
- Attacks that need the phone in someone else's hands unlocked, root, adb or a debuggable build. A bypass of the app passcode by another app, without these, is in scope.

## How to exercise it

The image has the checkout of `main` in `/src` with its submodules (one commit each; most of Chromium's `third_party` and test data are left out by the profile of `/opt/git-tools`), built by `./gradlew :app:assembleLatestDebug -Pabi=x64`: debug, flavor `latest` (Android 13 and newer), ABI x86_64 only, package `org.example.tgx`, signed by a generated debug key. It leaves:

- the APK in `app/build/outputs/apk/latest/debug/`;
- the native libraries with debug info, unstripped, in `app/build/intermediates/cxx/Debug/<hash>/obj/x86_64/`: `libtgxjni.so` (the media code and the highlighting of code of the app, with FFmpeg, libvpx, libwebp, opus, FLAC, libyuv, LZ4, libprisma, Boost.Regex and tlottie linked in) and `libtgcallsjni.so` (calls); their CMake tree with `build.ninja` and `compile_commands.json` in `app/.cxx/Debug/<hash>/x86_64/`;
- FFmpeg and libvpx as built by `BuildFfmpegTask` and `BuildLibvpxTask` with their own optimization flags and without debug info, in `app/build/generated/tgx/ffmpeg/latest/x86_64/` and `libvpx/latest/x86_64/` (logs in `ffmpeg-build/` and `libvpx-build/`), and tlottie in `app/build/generated/tgx/tlottie/latest/x86_64/`;
- the classes in `app/build/intermediates/javac/latestDebug/` and `built_in_kotlinc/latestDebug/`.

Gradle and Cargo are offline (`/root/.gradle/init.d/offline.gradle`, `/root/.cargo/config.toml`). After a change, `./gradlew --offline :app:assembleLatestDebug -Pabi=x64` rebuilds the same variant; `:app:compileLatestDebugJavaWithJavac -Pabi=x64` only Java; native code also rebuilds by `ninja -C app/.cxx/Debug/<hash>/x86_64` with the ninja of `$ANDROID_HOME/cmake/<version.cmake>/bin/`. The scan machine is smaller than the build machine: rebuild single tasks rather than everything; Gradle there takes the smaller heaps of `/root/.gradle/gradle.properties`. The Rust toolchain of `version.rust` is rustup's default.

What the image can't run: the app itself (no emulator, no device, no Google Play services, no Telegram servers, so no login, chats or calls), TDLib from source (prebuilt and stripped; its sources are in `tdlib/source/td` for reading), and tests (none are in this repository). So reproduce in a harness: native bugs with the library built for the host from the sources under `app/jni/third_party/` and driven the way `gif.cpp`, `image.c` or `voice.c` drive it, with the crafted file (for tlottie, `cargo run --offline --features cli --bin tlottie-cli -- render <file.json> <frame> <size> <out.png>` in `app/jni/third_party/tlottie`); Java bugs with the class on the JVM and Android stubbed, or a precise trace through the code. A report names the attacker (another user, a bot, a channel admin, a web page, another installed app, the other side of a call), what they send, what the victim has to do, the file and function, and a patch.

## How you rate severity

- **Critical**: code execution in the app, or reading or changing its private data (sessions, messages, files, settings), by another user, bot, channel or web page, when the victim only receives something or does something ordinary: opens a chat, a message, a link preview, Instant View or a sticker, or answers a call. Also anything that gives an account's session to someone else.
- **High**: memory corruption in native code reachable with content of others (stickers, emoji, GIFs, videos, voice and music, backgrounds, calls), even without a working exploit; another installed app without special permissions making Telegram X send, show, delete or hand over messages or files, or open a chat over the passcode of the app; Telegram X turning on a proxy, sharing a location, phone number or contacts, or logging in to a site without the confirmation its UI promises; content of secret chats written where other apps can read it.
- **Medium**: a crash that keeps coming back from content of others (the app or a chat can't be opened until the content is gone); spoofing that misleads a security decision, e.g. a link that shows one target and opens another; content that makes the app contact third-party servers against the user's settings, revealing the IP address; memory corruption reachable only from a file the user picks on the phone.
- **Low**: a crash that doesn't come back, from content of others or from an intent of another app; data of others in the app's own logs; hardening without a shown impact, e.g. settings of a web view.

Medium and Low go one level higher when nothing but receiving is needed (the chat list, notifications, automatic downloads), so a received message that crashes the app at every start is High. Anything goes one level lower when it needs a setting changed from its default or many steps of the victim. Memory corruption without a working exploit stays at most High. Give the severity with its reason, and don't inflate it.

## Anything to leave alone

- Keys and identifiers that are public by design: `app/google-services.json`, `app/agconnect-services.json`, the key of Google Maps in `app/src/main/AndroidManifest.xml`, the sample `telegram.api_id` and `telegram.api_hash` in `local.properties.sample`, and the names of channels in `buildSrc/src/main/kotlin/Config.kt`.
- What only this build is: debuggable, signed by a debug key, experimental (`BuildConfig.EXPERIMENTAL`, from the example package name), Firebase off. What only `BuildConfig.DEBUG` or `BuildConfig.EXPERIMENTAL` turns on, e.g. `Config.ALLOW_DEBUG_DC`, is not in official releases; report what holds in a release.
- Test hooks (`Config.ENABLE_TEST_HOOKS`, on only in the `nonMinifiedRelease` and `benchmarkRelease` build types that tests use) and tools of developer accounts (`Tdlib.isDeveloper()`), e.g. the session snapshot of `component/attach/MediaBottomFilesController.java`, which warns before it acts.
- Features Telegram X doesn't have, e.g. mini apps, payments, Telegram Passport and TON, whose links show as unsupported: their absence is no vulnerability.
- Data in the app's private storage that isn't encrypted by the app (files of TDLib, settings, caches): reaching it needs root or adb, and backups are off (`android:allowBackup="false"`). The passcode of the app locks its screens and doesn't encrypt data.
